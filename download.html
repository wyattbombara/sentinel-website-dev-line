Release notes

Sentinel 1.17.3 Argus

Historical notes imported from the source repository. They do not confirm an available download or functionality in this preview.

Polish for the newest features: Scan with Sentinel in the right-click menu now works without signing in, checking the file on your computer; fewer false alarms from the slow-scam, gift card pause and sender checks; and the browser companion's warnings fit small windows.

Read the imported development history +

1.17.2: more fixes: parts of the home and privacy pages that never appeared on phones and tablets now show, the download page fits a phone and its installer details no longer run together, email scan advice is written for emails, and the browser companion follows sites that change screens without reloading.

1.17.1: fixes and polish: the gold Sentinel name on the home page stays centred again, the pause before gift cards and crypto also recognises send and withdraw screens whose address says nothing, and the newest features look and read better on phones and in the light theme.

1.17.0: warnings about a fake copy of a site now offer one button to the real one. A calm pause appears before paying in gift cards or crypto when something scam-like happened shortly before. Email scan can tell who really sent a message from its headers, Text scan can check a whole conversation for the slow scams that build up over weeks, and Windows can scan any file from the right-click menu.

1.16.0: tick what a caller is asking for and see at once whether to hang up. Email scans now mark each warning sign inside the email itself. After a scam is found, Warn a friend makes a picture of it that is safe to share. A practice inbox on the website lets you sort scams from real messages. And if you leave a flagged sign-in page, Sentinel asks once whether you typed anything and shows what to do if you did.

1.15.0: a calm word before you pay a shop whose address is only weeks old, a catch for fake installers that came from the wrong site, a weekly recap of what Sentinel caught for you, and one key (Windows key + Alt + S) to check anything on your screen.

1.14.0: point at any link on any page and a small mask says whether it is safe. A new Text scan page checks text messages and phone screenshots. Wallet guard catches malware that swaps the crypto address you copy, and Sentinel now learns the sites you really use and warns about fake copies of them. Fixed: the warning window for dangerous pages shows again, and the Windows app no longer pauses for seconds at a time.

1.13.1: smoother scrolling: the website glides with a mouse wheel, its chapter animations follow your scroll in both directions, a band of light crosses the gold wordmark, and the big gold buttons lean toward your pointer. Phones keep their own scrolling, and the reduce-motion setting turns it all off.

1.13.0: a new way to move. On the website, your mouse peels the home page back to show what Sentinel sees underneath, chapters open with a gold laser line, headings rise letter by letter and stats count up. In the app, messages arrive as an island that springs open and morphs, and cards pop in with a spring. In the Windows app, the corner mask opens into its message. Everything respects the reduce-motion setting.

1.12.3: exposure alerts now also hear about single scam pages from the fastest phishing lists, the tech-support scam shield asks when an already running remote-control program gets a new connection, the browser companion tells you once when a password is protected, the browser checkup reads Edge correctly, and if Sentinel ever fails to start it now says why.

1.12.2: polish for the ten new protections, after every one was tested on a real Windows machine: QR codes no longer send Wi-Fi passwords or sign-in secrets anywhere, the website’s Try a link no longer claims more than it checked, the tech-support shield closes installed remote tools properly, ordinary code snippets and bank texts are no longer flagged, and the browser checkup reads Opera and Firefox’s search engine without flagging Firefox’s own add-ons.

1.12.1: chat safety now really reads Roblox and Discord (its reader had been stopping the moment it started), Roblox warnings stay on screen, and Stop pasted commands and Check links I copy now act on what you copy. Every 1.12.0 feature was run for real on a Windows machine before this release.

1.12.0: ten new protections:

  • The tech-support scam shield gets you out of fake virus screens, and asks before a stranger takes over your PC.
  • The QR code checker says where a code really goes, and catches Discord and WhatsApp sign-in traps.
  • The browser companion has a password alarm: it stops you typing a protected password into the wrong site.
  • The recovery guide puts what to do in order if you think you have been scammed.
  • Check my texts warns about scam texts in Phone Link.
  • Stop pasted commands catches the commands fake “I am not a robot” pages ask you to paste.
  • The browser checkup reads your browsers’ add-ons and settings and points out the risky ones.
  • Exposure alerts tell you if a site you visited turns out to be dangerous.
  • Try a link on the website now runs Sentinel’s own address checks.
  • The parent lock asks for a PIN before anyone switches Sentinel’s protection off.

1.11.1: delicate scanning digs in much more binary, for longer, and live scanning’s marks pop in once instead of bouncing after every scroll. Chat safety now flags a phone number said in Roblox or Discord, and one from the area codes known for “one ring” and callback scams as a scam. It offers itself when Roblox or Discord opens, has a tray switch, and Live protection shows how many messages it has checked.

1.11.0: delicate scanning digs in binary: after the gold line, your results corrupt into flickering gold binary for a moment, each result’s digits taking its colour until its verdict is in, then it fades. Manual link scans now come as Fast or Delicate (which researches the site): Free has 10 fast a week, Pro 200 fast and 40 delicate, Max 1,000 and 100, Ultimate unlimited fast and 500 delicate.

1.10.0: new: chat safety for Roblox and the Discord app. Sentinel points out scams and people who may not be safe to talk to (someone asking a child to keep secrets, send pictures, move to another app or meet), right beside the message, judging each chat by the game or server it is in. It all happens on your computer: nothing is sent, saved or logged, nothing is read while a game’s chat is closed, and in a Roblox game the badge shows only in the Esc menu. It is off until you, or a parent, turn it on.

Live scanning’s marks now follow the page by its pixels as it scrolls, with a wheel, a touchpad or the scrollbar, and a safe result gets a green mask. The website is much lighter: one renderer draws every 3D mask, the hero turns calmly with darker silver and glowing eyes, and a tab now sights its chapter through a scope.

1.9.12: more email scams are caught: task-job offers that promise daily pay for rating products, fake voicemail notices behind a link that will “expire”, and refunds that first want your card details. Real gig ads and office voicemail emails are left alone.

1.9.11: free pages named for a brand with a lure after it, and pages posing as a TinyURL or Bitly link, are caught, while pages named in ordinary words are left alone. 97.0% of today’s phishing addresses are flagged, up from 95.7%, with no new false alarms.

1.9.10: sites imitating 25 more of the most impersonated names are caught, among them Navy Federal, Scotiabank, TD, Disney+, Hulu, Max, TurboTax, Schwab, Etsy, Xbox and Battle.net. Their fan and companion sites are left alone.

1.9.9: the reasons in each threat plate’s example read on full lines again, the app’s usage and history labels match the rest of the app, and the Free plan’s card lists its 15 minutes a week of fast live scanning.

1.9.8: pages about a company, such as reviews, news articles, how-to guides, forums and store listings, are no longer marked as pretending to be it, so live scanning stops marking them yellow in search results. Places named for a sponsor, such as an arena, are left alone, while a bank’s name in a folder beside a sign-in folder is caught. All 111 real sites with brand-like names are now left alone, and 95.7% of today’s phishing addresses are caught (up from 94.7%).

1.9.7: Sentinel catches more scams from the address alone: unpaid-toll texts naming E-ZPass, SunPass, TxTag and other toll agencies, letters swapped for their twins (paypai, robloxx), sites spelled to read like .gov, fake browser updates, sites named for “I am not a robot” checks, free Robux bait, and fake Norton, McAfee, Geek Squad, Ticketmaster and password manager sites. Of 80 addresses written the way these scams are, 77 are now flagged, up from 57. Names with a number in them, such as 1password.com and 23andme.com, are no longer read as disguised, and 434 real sites and 145 real sign-in, account and download pages were checked with no new false alarms.

1.9.6: the threat plates hold still as you switch severity, the black hole no longer bends the mask before it appears, and the site loads faster and runs lighter. The top of the S in SENTINEL is no longer cut off.

1.9.5: the black hole on the threat plates now consumes the mask, growing from the centre and dragging the metal in, after the ring joining the scope’s diamonds lights up. It bends the starlight further as it grows, and it is much lighter on your computer.

1.9.4: the 3D masks showed the inside of their shell instead of their outside. They now look like solid cast masks, lit from the front, with the eyes set into them.

1.9.3: the masks are real 3D models now, sharp at any size. The black mask turns to watch your pointer with glowing eyes, and choosing a severity on a threat plate opens a black hole that swallows the old mask and gives back the new one. The five models are listed below, and your model is shown under the app’s logo.

1.9.2: Sentinel’s versions are models now, named for guardians from myth: this is Argus. A picker under the update check lists every Argus version and switches to the one you choose. Screenshots of emails can be dropped in and are read on your computer, never uploaded. And the masks on the site now watch your pointer, and look away when you leave.

1.9.1: Sentinel now opens on a sheet of warm paper, where a pencil sketch of the page draws itself, line by line, for as long as the page takes to load. When the sketch is complete it becomes the page: the paper dissolves, the pencil fades and the colours wash in.

1.9.0: the app now wears the same black lacquer and gold as the site. Every page has an entrance of its own, a gold sweep crosses the top as you change pages, titles rise over a rule that draws itself, and panels catch a light that follows your pointer. Sentinel opens with its black mask arriving as glowing gold cells that resolve into the real render. New passwords are checked against known breaches, and scans can no longer hang on a slow site.

1.8.9: a new look, in black lacquer and gold. The three masks are real 3D renders now, and each one turns yellow, orange or red as you pick how sure Sentinel is. The pages carry Victorian frames, pencil construction sketches, raised panels and a slow mist of light, every section arrives its own way, and clicking a tab sweeps you to its chapter.

1.8.8: A new look: editorial serif headlines, the gold gradient back on the buttons, soft raised panels and a new way for sections to arrive as you scroll. Real sites named like big companies are left alone (110 of 111, up from 106), and a site’s front page is no longer blamed for a listed page elsewhere on it.

1.8.7: fixes from a full review of the last six updates: hidden PowerShell downloaders and phishing forms with little scripts in them are caught again, real security mail stays unmarked, marks come back after a wheel turned over something that does not scroll, and Restore keeps a backup of anything it cannot put back.

1.8.6: the file scanner leaves your real programs alone: measured on 4,500 real files from Windows and the programs installed with it, none is marked. Scripts are only treated as downloaders when they both fetch and run something, and a program or archive hiding behind a document name is still caught.

1.8.5: email scams are caught even when they have no link: callback scams (“call this number to cancel”), fake tech support, gift-card and wire requests from a “boss”, requests for your bank details or Social Security number, and toll and fine demands. On 80 real emails from real companies and 46 of today’s scams, every scam was flagged and no real email was.

1.8.4: more phishing caught from the address alone (96.3% of today’s phishing addresses, up from 95.0%): pages hidden behind generated folder names, throwaway site-builder pages and web hosts’ temporary addresses are now flagged. Shared Google documents are no longer marked yellow, and Sentinel is now measured against 99 real pages deep inside real sites, with no false alarms.

1.8.3: Sentinel is gentler on your PC. It only listens to your mouse while live scanning has marks on screen, never starts an update over a fullscreen game, and asks Microsoft Defender before acting on a file it cannot read. Restore now brings back Startup shortcuts and scheduled tasks too, and never overwrites a newer file.

1.8.2: marks stay on their results when you keep scrolling at the end of a page or scroll an inbox list, and a slow browser no longer throws them back mid-scroll. Accounts are safer: Google sign-in finishes only in the browser that started it, turning on two-factor asks for your password, and community reports need confirmed, week-old accounts. Light mode is readable everywhere, every page links the Privacy Policy, Terms and Refund Policy, and the browser companion (1.4.1) matches the website.

1.8.1: marks move the moment your mouse wheel turns, along the same curve as the page, and positions read mid-scroll no longer throw them off the page. Sentinel reads each result’s title and your search words to understand a site without opening it, and reads names made of real words as words, so overdrive.com is no longer mistaken for OneDrive while look-alikes such as onedrlve are still caught.

The website has light and dark themes, new fonts made for reading, a Refund Policy, and clearer Privacy and Terms. Other browser extensions can no longer read your account through the site.

1.7.0 brought together the 1.6.11 to 1.6.18 updates: Google results and ads checked, inbox scams caught, downloads checked in the background, much less work in the background, and live scanning tested in Edge, Chrome and Firefox.
